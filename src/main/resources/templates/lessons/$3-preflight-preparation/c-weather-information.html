Objective

  • The learner will understand weather products, weather theory, and weather information used for flight planning.
  • The learner will develop skills to acquire, read, and interpret weather information and make decisions related to flight safety.
  • The learner will mitigate risk by recognizing hazardous weather and practicing making go / no go decisions.

AIRMETs, SIGMETs, and Convective SIGMETs

AIRMET

  • 6 hr
    • SIERRA - mountain obscuration
    • TANGO - turbulence
    • ZULU - icing

SIGMET

  • 4 hr
  • severe icing, extreme turbulence, sandstorms, volcanic ash

Conv SIGMET

  • 2 hr
  • severe, embedded, or squall line thunderstorms
  • surface winds greater than 50 knots
  • hail greater than 3/4 in
  • tornadoes

Atmospheric Composition and Stability

Atmospheric Composition

  • Nitrogen - 78%
  • Oxygen - 21%
  • Other - 1%

Atmospheric Layers

  • Troposphere
    • surface to about FL360 on average
    • temperature decreases with height
    • most weather happens below the tropopause
  • Stratosphere
    • temperature increases with height
    • commercial aircraft cruise here to avoid turbulence
  • Mesosphere
    • decrease in temperature with height
  • Thermosphere
    • temperature increases with height
  • Exosphere

Standard Atmosphere

  • 29.92" Hg / 1013.25 mb
