Foundations of Effective Assessment

Effective Assessment provides practical and specific feedback on learner progress.

Effective Assessment is useful for both the instructor and learner.

Instructor
  • evaluate student's level of understanding
  • recognize parts of instruction that need more emphasis
Learner
  • recognize parts of understanding and skills that need more emphasis
  • provide learner with guidance to improve
  • provide learner with opportunities to improve self-assessment

Characteristics of Effective Assessment

  • Objective
    • free of opinion or bias
    • avoid "halo error"
  • Flexible
    • adjust the tone, technique, content, depth to fit the circumstances
  • Acceptable
    • learners will more readily accept critique from evaluators they deem qualified
    • demonstrate authority, conviction, sincerity, competence
  • Comprehensive
    • cover strengths and deficiencies
    • tailor evaluation to provide the most benefit
  • Constructive
    • learner should benefit and progress
    • stay positive
  • Organized
    • follow a logical pattern
  • Thoughtful
    • consider the learners need for self-esteem, recognition, approval
  • Specific
    • learner should have a clear understanding of strengths and deficiencies

Traditional vs Authentic Assessment

Traditional

  • written tests: multiple choice, matching, fill in the blank

Authentic

  • demonstrate real-world tasks
  • uses open ended questions, but still with established performance criteria

Types of Authentic Assessment

Learner Centered Assessment
  • replay - replay the scene
  • reconstruct - identify what could have been done differently
  • reflect - gain insight
  • redirect - relate to other solutions and experiences
Maneuver or Procedure Skills
  • describe - can describe elements and steps, but cannot execute
  • explain - can describe concepts and principles, but cannot execute
  • practice - can plan and execute, instructor assists with errors
  • perform - can properly execute, no instructor input
Assessing Risk Management Skills
  • explain - can identify and explain elements of risk, needs prompting to identify and make decisions
  • practice - can identify and apply SRM principles, instructor assists with errors
  • perform - can identify and evaluate risk and make corrective decisions, no instructor input

Choosing an Effective Assessment Method

  • determine level and domain of learning
  • list measurable indicators of proper execution
  • establish criteria objectives
  • develop criterion-reference test items

Critique

Instructor provides perspective and feedback on the good and bad to help the learner improve.

The goal is to leave the learner with a clear and confident path to improve.

Types of Critique

  • Instructor / Learner
  • Learner Led
  • Small Group
  • Critique by Another Learner
  • Self-Critique
  • Written

Ground Rules

  • do not extend critique beyond scheduled time
  • do not try to cover too much
  • do not end without a summary to reemphasize the main points
  • do not use absolute statements
  • do not engage in classroom controversies or take sides
  • do not allow yourself to be maneuvered into defending criticism
  • do not have discrepancies between written and oral portions

Oral Assessment

  • test both rote knowledge and HOTS
  • catches gaps in understanding
  • encourages interest and participation

Effective Questions

  • applicable
  • brief, concise, clear, definite
  • adapted to the level of the learner
  • one idea at a time (WWWWWH, but not combined)
  • challenging

avoid: binary, puzzle, oversize, toss-up, bewilderment, trick, irrelevant

Learner Questions

  • ensure understanding
  • display interest
  • confirm satisfaction with the answer
  • if too advanced, explain and introduce later
  • if you don't know the answer, admit it, and follow up

Assessing Piloting Ability

During Flight

  • safety permitting, allow students to make mistakes, and teach how to correct
  • vary situations to ensure that the student understands principles

After Flight

  • postflight evaluation
  • allow students to self-assess

Over Time

  • transfer responsibility to students
  • demand demonstrated ability and consistent proficiency before solo
  • always maintain positive, confidence building experience
