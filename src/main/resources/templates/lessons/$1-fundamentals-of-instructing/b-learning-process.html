Learning How to Learn

Definitions of Learning

A change in behavior as a result of experience.

Two Theories

Behaviorism
  • focused on overt behavior
  • learning is a response to environmental stimuli (rewards and punishments)
  • Pavlov's dog
Cognitive Theory
  • focused on functions and processes of the mind
  • Bloom's Taxonomy: knowledge, comprehension, application, analysis, synthesis, evaluation
  • Information Processing Theory
    • the brain processes information like a computer
    • habits and routines become automatic (rudder)
  • Constructivism
    • learners build new understanding using already acquired knowledge and experiences
    • instructors accommodate this by knowing what the learner already understands
  • High Order Thinking Skills (HOTS)
    • beyond rote memorization (analysis, synthesis, evaluation)
    • we call it ADM in aviation
    • trained through SBT

How We Construct Meaning

Perceptions

  • all learning comes from perceptions via our senses
  • Factors that Affect Perception
    • physical organism
    • goals and values
    • self-concept
    • time and opportunity
    • element of threat

Insight

  • The result of grouping perceptions into meaningful wholes.

The instructor's responsibility is to facilitate insights.

Principles of Learning

best conditions to promote learning

Thorndike's Laws of Learning

  • Readiness: students learn best when they are ready to learn
  • Effect: learning is strengthened by a positive emotional feeling
  • Exercise: things most often repeated are best remembered
  • Primacy: what is learned first creates a strong impression
  • Intensity: a dramatic or exciting learning experience teaches more than a boring one
  • Recency: things most recently learned are best remembered

Characteristics of Learning

  • Result of Experience: learning has to come from personal experiences
  • Active Process: learning requires a response
  • Multifaceted: learning combines conceptual, emotional, motor skills, decisions, etc.
  • Purposeful: learning furthers goals

Transfer of Learning

Positive

Learning one thing helps learn another

slow flight -> short field landings

Negative

Learning one thing hinders another

inverted video game controls -> steering during taxi with pedals

Acquiring Skills

Skill knowledge often cannot easily be put into words. Rather it is gained through experience.

Stages of Skill Acquisition

  • Cognitive Stage
    • learner memorizes a clear of steps
    • often unaware of progress
  • Associative Stage
    • learner practices the skill
    • incorporates specific, small adjustments
    • aware of progress
  • Automatic Response Stage
    • skill becomes second nature, can be done "by feel"
    • learner can often do other tasks simultaneously or have a conversation
    • learner often forgets the individual steps or can no longer explain how to perform the maneuver

Knowledge of Results

Learners will often be aware that something is wrong, but will not know how to correct it. A critical responsibility of an instructor is to teach a student to recognize and how to correct their errors.

How to Develop Skills

  • practice improves performance

Learning Plateaus

Temporary leveling off of learning.

Learning Plateaus are normal.

Mitigate by moving to a different maneuver or giving the student a break

Acquiring Skills

Types of Practice

  • Deliberate: aimed at a specific goal with feedback
  • Blocked: repeating the same drill over and over (leads to good short-term performance, poor long-term retention)
  • Random: mixing up maneuvers (better for long-term retention)

Scenario-Based Training (SBT)

Using realistic situations to teach decision-making rather than just isolated maneuvers.

  • clear objectives
  • tailored to the needs of the learner
  • capitalizes on the nuances of the local environment

Memory, Forgetting, and Retention

Memory Systems

Sensory Register

  • receives input from senses
  • discards or passes it to STM in seconds

Short-Term Memory

  • holds information for about 30 seconds
  • limited capacity: approx 7 items

Long-Term Memory

  • permanent storage

Retention of Learning

  • praise
  • favorable attitude
  • association
  • meaningful repetition
  • multi-sensory learning

Why We Forget

  • retrieval failure
  • interference
  • fading
  • repression
